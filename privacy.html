Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 27, 2026

This policy explains what data Jira Agent for Slack (“the app”, “we”) collects when you install and use it, why we collect it, how long we keep it, who processes it, and how you can access or delete it.

Questions or requests about your data: support@YOUR-DOMAIN.

1. Summary

  • We store only what the app needs to work: Slack and Jira identifiers, encrypted access tokens, and the worklog requests you create.
  • We cannot read your Slack messages, channels or files.
  • We do not sell data, show ads, or use your data to train AI models.
  • You can delete your data at any time with /jira-agent disconnect, or by uninstalling the app.

2. Data we collect and why

DataWhy
Workspace installation: Slack workspace ID and name, app and bot IDs, the bot access token, granted permissions, the Slack user ID of the person who installed the app, installation time. To answer commands and send messages in your workspace. The bot token and workspace name are encrypted.
Jira connection (per Slack user): Slack workspace and user IDs, Jira account ID, Jira site ID, URL and name, Jira OAuth access and refresh tokens, token expiry, granted Jira permissions, your time zone, connection times. To call Jira on your behalf, build links to your Jira site, and compute “today” in your time zone. Tokens and the site URL and name are encrypted.
Worklog requests: issue key, date, start time, duration, description and remaining-estimate choice you enter; your Slack workspace, user and channel IDs; status and timestamps. To show you the approval message, write the worklog to Jira only after you approve it, block duplicate submissions, and keep an audit trail.
Sign-in links: a random one-time code with your Slack workspace and user IDs. To protect the “Add to Slack” and “Connect Jira” flows against forged requests.
Usage counters: number of assistant requests per user per hour. To limit abuse of the optional AI assistant.
Technical logs: Slack workspace and user IDs, request types, error types and times. To operate the service and fix problems. Logs never contain tokens, Slack request bodies or worklog descriptions.

Data read from Jira to answer a command (ticket summaries, statuses, worklogs) is shown to you in Slack and is not stored.

3. Data we receive but do not use

Slack sends every app some information with each command or button click, such as your Slack user name, the channel name and the workspace domain. The app does not store or use these values. When you look up your Jira profile, Atlassian may also return details such as your display name or e-mail address; the app stores only your Jira account ID and time zone.

4. Data we never access

  • Slack messages, channel history, files or direct messages (the app does not have these permissions).
  • Slack or Atlassian passwords: sign-in happens on Slack's and Atlassian's own pages.
  • Payment or financial information.

When the app is uninstalled from a workspace, we delete that workspace's installation, every Jira connection in that workspace, and all pending worklog requests automatically.

6. Where data is processed

  • Amazon Web Services (AWS Lambda, DynamoDB, KMS, CloudWatch) in the Asia Pacific (Singapore) region hosts the app and stores the data described above.
  • Atlassian receives the requests the app makes to Jira on your behalf.
  • Slack receives the messages the app sends you.

We do not sell, rent or share your data with anyone else, except when required by law.

7. Security

  • All traffic uses HTTPS (TLS 1.2 or newer). Every request from Slack is checked with Slack's signing secret.
  • Tokens and site names are encrypted with AWS KMS, each bound to its own record; databases are also encrypted at rest.
  • Tokens are never written to logs, never shown in Slack, and never returned to your browser.
  • Only the person who created a worklog request can approve it, and each request can be used once.

8. Access and deletion

You can, at any time:

  • Delete your Jira connection and tokens: type /jira-agent disconnect in Slack.
  • Revoke Jira access at Atlassian: Atlassian account settings → Connected apps → remove Jira Agent.
  • Remove everything for your workspace: a Slack admin uninstalls the app (Slack → Apps → Jira Agent → Remove).
  • Ask for a copy, a correction or deletion of any other data (for example your worklog request history), under GDPR, CCPA or similar laws: e-mail support@YOUR-DOMAIN with your Slack workspace name. We answer within 30 days, usually much sooner.

Worklogs already written to Jira belong to your Jira site; delete them in Jira if needed.

9. AI features

The optional natural-language assistant is turned off. If it is turned on in the future, the text you type after /jira-agent will be sent to Amazon Bedrock in the same AWS region to understand your request. It will not be stored by the app or used to train models, the assistant will never write to Jira without your approval, and this policy will be updated before the change.
